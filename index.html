(04) How we work

Four steps.
No surprises.

011–2 weeks

Discovery

We talk to your users, dig into the existing system, and write down what "done" means. You get a scoped plan with a fixed price and a real timeline — not a vague estimate.

  • Stakeholder interviews
  • Technical audit
  • Scope & roadmap
022–4 weeks

Design

Wireframes, then real interface design, then a prototype you can put in front of customers. Engineers sit in every review so nothing gets designed that can't be built.

  • UX flows & wireframes
  • UI & design system
  • Usability testing
034–12 weeks

Build

Two-week sprints, a demo at the end of every one, and a staging environment you can open at any time. Tests, code review and CI aren't optional extras.

  • Weekly demos
  • Automated testing
  • Staging from week one
04Ongoing

Launch & grow

We handle the release, monitor the first weeks closely, then keep improving with real usage data. Most of our clients stay with us for years.

  • Release management
  • Monitoring & on-call
  • Roadmap & retainer

(07) Questions

Things people ask
before they call.

How much does a project cost?

Most engagements land between €25k and €250k. After a paid discovery phase (from €4k) you get a fixed price and a timeline. We don't do open-ended hourly work on new builds.

Who will actually work on my project?

A dedicated team of 2–6 people: a lead engineer, designers and developers, plus a producer who runs the schedule. You talk to them directly in a shared Slack channel — no account managers in between.

Do you work with existing codebases?

Yes — about a third of our work is taking over, stabilising and extending systems built by someone else. We start with a technical audit so you know what you have before we touch it.

Which time zones do you cover?

The core team is in Central Europe with overlap from 8:00 to 20:00 CET. We've run projects with clients in New York, Dubai and Singapore without anyone waking up at 4am.

Who owns the code?

You do, from the first commit. Everything lives in your repositories and your cloud accounts. If you ever want to leave, you take everything with you — that's the deal.
